## SECTION 1: BUSINESS OVERVIEW

### What BIO.OS Sells

BIO.OS is a surgical intelligence operating system — not a robot, but the software that makes surgical robots resilient, predictable, and accessible. The company licenses its operating system to hospitals, sells hardware kits that run the OS, offers digital twin subscriptions for predictive surgery, and charges for implementation and training.

The business model is deliberately capital-light. Instead of building million-dollar proprietary robots, BIO.OS runs on Raspberry Pi CM4 hardware costing less than fifty thousand dollars per system. This lowers the barrier to entry for hospitals and enables rapid scaling.

### Who the Customer Is

BIO.OS serves three customer segments:

First, Tier-1 hospitals that need a safety-critical backup layer for their existing surgical robots. These hospitals buy BIO.OS as redundancy insurance — if their primary da Vinci or Hugo system fails due to power or network issues, BIO.OS takes over seamlessly.

Second, low-to-middle-income country hospitals and rural clinics that have never had access to robotic surgery because current systems require clean power, fiber-optic networks, and million-dollar budgets. BIO.OS tolerates power fluctuations, packet loss, and runs on low-cost hardware.

Third, NGOs and government health programs (WHO, Gates Foundation, USAID) that deploy surgical capacity in underserved regions. These organizations purchase BIO.OS deployment packages for field hospitals and disaster relief.

## SECTION 2: REVENUE STREAMS

BIO.OS generates revenue through four distinct streams:

### Stream 1: BIO.OS License

An annual per-operating-system license costing fifteen thousand to fifty thousand dollars per hospital per year. This is the core recurring revenue. Hospitals pay for the right to run the BIO.OS operating system on their surgical hardware. The license includes software updates, security patches, and access to the digital twin prediction service.

### Stream 2: Hardware Kit

A physical kit costing twenty-five thousand to forty thousand dollars per kit. Each kit includes a Raspberry Pi CM4 board, three independent microcontrollers, and a supercapacitor power buffer board. Hospitals that do not already have compatible hardware purchase the kit to run BIO.OS. This is one-time revenue per installation.

### Stream 3: Digital Twin Subscription

A predictive simulation service costing five thousand to fifteen thousand dollars per hospital per year. The digital twin runs in parallel with live surgery and predicts complications thirty seconds before they occur. Hospitals subscribe to this service for the predictive safety layer — essentially paying for advance warning of surgical complications.

### Stream 4: Implementation and Training

One-time deployment, integration, and surgeon training costing ten thousand to twenty-five thousand dollars per installation. This includes on-site setup, integration with existing hospital systems, and training surgeons and nurses to use BIO.OS effectively. This is services revenue that supports the initial rollout.
